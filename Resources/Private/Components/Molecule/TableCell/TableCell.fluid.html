TableCell — one `td`. `truncate` and `wrap` are the two answers to a value that is longer than its column, and they are opposites, so a cell takes one or the other. Truncating is the stricter one and it works by a trick worth knowing about before you use it: the rule sets `max-inline-size: 0` so that the cell stops claiming its content's width, and the column then sizes from the other rows. On a table whose every cell truncates, the columns have nothing left to size from and collapse to equal widths. `colspan`, `rowspan` and `headers` are written only when they have a value. An empty `colspan` happens to parse as 1, but an empty `headers` is a cell that claims to belong to no heading at all, and none of the three is conforming markup. `headers` is for the tables that `scope` cannot describe: two levels of column headings, a cell governed by a heading that is neither at the top of its column nor at the start of its row. It takes the ids of the `th`s this cell belongs to, space-separated, and TableHeaderCell takes the `id` to put on them. Most tables need neither; a table that needs this needs it everywhere, because a partial `headers` graph is worse than none. `emphasis="accent"` marks the cells of one column out from the rest: the plan or the product a comparison is arguing for. Put it on every cell of that column, the header cell included — the table cannot know which column a cell is in. The tint is a light wash of the accent, so text and links in it keep their contrast; it is a highlight, not a status, and the column's heading still has to say what is special about it.
