TableCell — one `td`. `truncate` and `wrap` are the two answers to a value that is longer than its column, and they are opposites, so a cell takes one or the other. Truncating is the stricter one and it works by a trick worth knowing about before you use it: the rule sets `max-inline-size: 0` so that the cell stops claiming its content's width, and the column then sizes from the other rows. On a table whose every cell truncates, the columns have nothing left to size from and collapse to equal widths. `colspan` and `rowspan` are emitted whether or not they are set. An empty one is not a valid non-negative integer, so the parser falls back to the attribute's own default of 1 — which is exactly what an unset argument should mean, and it saves a second copy of the tag for each of them. `headers` is for the tables that `scope` cannot describe: two levels of column headings, a cell governed by a heading that is neither at the top of its column nor at the start of its row. It takes the ids of the `th`s this cell belongs to, space-separated, and TableHeaderCell takes the `id` to put on them. Most tables need neither; a table that needs this needs it everywhere, because a partial `headers` graph is worse than none.
