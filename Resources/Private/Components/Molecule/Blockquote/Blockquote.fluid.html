Blockquote — a quotation set apart from the text around it. Only the words go in here. The person who said them is a Citation, placed after this element inside a Figure, because HTML is explicit that an attribution is not part of the quotation: putting the name inside the
makes a screen reader read it as something the speaker said about themselves. The accent rule down the inline start edge is the quotation mark. There is no decorative glyph by default, so a quote still reads as a quote when the stylesheet fails and when it is copied into plain text. A bare paragraph inside takes the quote's voice: its size, face, weight and colour, and no margins of its own. A Text atom inside keeps the type it was given. `size` is a scale, each step a clear move up from the last: default the large step at body weight — one quote among several lg the large step at its own weight — a featured quote xl the heading face at the third heading size, balanced — a pull quote; `large` is the older name for the same step display the third display step, balanced and fluid on a phone — a quote that is the whole band `variant="plain"` drops the accent rule and its indent, for a quote that already sits apart — on a card, under a portrait, alone in a band. `marks="auto"` sets the quotation marks around the words, drawn by the stylesheet in the marks of the page's language: “…” in English, „…“ in German, «…» in French. The marks follow the `lang` of the page or of any ancestor, so a translated page needs nothing from the template, and they stay out of the editable text, where an editor would type them a second time. They mark the first and the last paragraph of the quote, or the quote's own text when it has no paragraph. `data-variant` and `data-marks` are written only when they are asked for.
