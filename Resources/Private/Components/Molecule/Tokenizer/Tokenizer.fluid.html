Tokenizer — a field whose value is a set of chips: tags on an article, recipients on a message, filters already applied. What is rendered is the honest half of the upstream control. The chips are real — they are what the server already knows — and the entry field is real, and a normal form submit is what turns what was typed into the next chip. What is not here is the part that makes it feel instant: nothing turns text into a chip as you type a comma, because that is script, and nothing removes a chip on click for the same reason. A caller that wants removal puts a submit button in each chip and lets the server do it, which works in every browser and costs a page load. The chips arrive through the slot as Token atoms rather than being rendered here from an array. A token is often a link — to the tag's own page — and often the current one, and a component that built them out of strings could express neither. The box is `cursor: text` and the whole of it is inside the field border, so clicking the empty space to the right of the last chip puts the caret where it looks like it should go. That much works without script.
