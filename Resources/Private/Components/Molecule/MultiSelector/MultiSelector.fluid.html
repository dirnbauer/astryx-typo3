Open, closed, Escape and the whole keyboard are the browser's, and the rows inside are real checkboxes — CheckboxListItem is what belongs in the slot — so the choices post with the form and survive a reload with nothing running on the page. What it is not is a `role="listbox" aria-multiselectable`. That role is a promise about the arrow keys: one tab stop, focus roving between options, space to select. All three are script. A listbox that does not answer the arrow keys is worse for somebody on a screen reader than a disclosure that behaves exactly as it announces itself, so the disclosure is what is rendered. `summaryLabel` is the line on the trigger and is the caller's to compose — "3 selected", "Vienna, Graz, Linz", "Any region". This component cannot count what is in its own slot, and a trigger that said "Select…" over three ticked boxes would be the one part of the control that lies. The panel is absolutely positioned so opening it does not push the next field down the form, and it stays in document order so the keyboard walks into it straight after the trigger.
