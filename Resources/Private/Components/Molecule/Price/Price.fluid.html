Price — an amount, what period it is for, and the price it replaces. Eight pricing elements built this line by hand, and the part they each had to remember is the part a reader never sees: a struck-through number is read out by a screen reader as just another number, so "€ 59 € 39 per month" sounds like two prices and no offer. When there is a former price, each figure is preceded by a visually hidden label — "Regular price", "Current price" by default, in the site's language — and the former one is an ``, the element for something that is no longer accurate. The parts are named slots as well as arguments, and the slot wins, as on SectionIntro: a slot carries the markup f:render.text returns, which is what makes a price editable on the page; a string argument would arrive escaped. The amount may also be the default slot when no part is named. amount the price, at a display size and in tabular digits period after it on its baseline: "/ month", "per seat", "one-off" former the price it replaces, struck through, before the amount `size` sets the amount — `sm` display-3, `md` display-2, `lg` display-1 — and the others follow it down the scale: the former price one display step smaller, so the saving still reads at a glance, and the period at the large text size (the label size beside a small amount). `align` centres the line (`center`) for a centred plan card. `as="div"` is for a price inside something that cannot hold a paragraph; the default is a `p`, because a price is a sentence: "€ 39 per month". `formerLabel` and `currentLabel` replace the hidden labels, for a former price that is not a regular one: "Bought separately", "Launch price". Whether there is a former price decides whether the amounts need their hidden labels. {formerName} {former} {currentName} {amount} {period}
