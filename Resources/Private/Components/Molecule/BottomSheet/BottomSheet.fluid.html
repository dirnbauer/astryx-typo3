BottomSheet — the panel that rises from the bottom edge of a phone. A `dialog` again, pinned to the block-end edge and as wide as the viewport, so everything a modal needs is the element's: the top layer, the backdrop, Escape, and focus that cannot wander into the page behind it. What is different is only where it comes from and where it stops, and both are stylesheet. The entry is a translate under `@starting-style`, which is what lets it slide in from `display: none` without a class being toggled at the right moment — and the whole slide is switched off under prefers-reduced-motion at the foot of the partial, where the sheet simply appears. "Switched off" means the duration goes to zero, never that the sheet stops opening: a reader who asked for less motion still gets the panel. There is no drag handle. The grabber pill is the affordance every native sheet has, and dragging it needs pointer events and script; a pill that invites a drag and ignores it is worse than no pill. The sheet is dismissed by Escape, by the backdrop and by the cross in its header, all of which work as drawn. The header, the body and the footer are the dialog family's own classes rather than three new ones. A sheet is a dialog that arrives from a different direction, and giving it a second recipe for a title row is how the two stop looking alike. {id}-title
