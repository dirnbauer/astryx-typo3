MoreMenu — the three dots at the end of a row, and what they open. The only component in the family that owns its trigger, because the trigger is the component: three dots, a name nobody can see, and a menu. Handing those back to the caller would mean every call site writing the same `popovertarget`, the same `aria-haspopup` and the same glyph, and getting the `aria-label` wrong once. The button is written out rather than composed from IconButton, and that is a real cost — IconButton declares no `popovertarget`, Fluid drops what a component does not declare, and an invoker without it is a dot that does nothing. Rather than widen that atom's surface for one caller, the button borrows its class and its two data attributes. No `aria-expanded` is written. A popover invoker gets the expanded state from the browser as the panel opens and closes; a hand-written one would be a string that never changes, and `aria-expanded="false"` on an open menu is worse than the attribute's absence. `aria-haspopup="menu"` is written, because that part is a fact about the button rather than a state. The dots need a name — "More actions for the September invoice" beats "More", which is what every one of them is called when nobody passes `label`.
