HoverCard — the preview panel that opens from a name, an avatar or a link. Tooltip's bigger sibling, and like Tooltip it renders the whole arrangement: the anchor, the trigger and the card. Leaving the anchor to the caller was the old answer and it could not work — a caller is a template, templates may not write `astryx-` classes, and the reveal in the stylesheet is written entirely against `.astryx-hover-card-anchor`. The anchor is a `div` and not a `span`, which is the one concession here: the card holds an avatar, a line of prose and a link, so the card is a `div`, and a `div` inside a `span` is not valid. `display: inline-flex` keeps the anchor sitting in its line either way. The cost is that a hover card does not belong in the middle of a paragraph of editor text. It is not `role="tooltip"`. A tooltip may not contain anything interactive, and a reader has to be able to move into this one. It has no role at all, therefore, and is announced through the trigger's `aria-describedby` — which is why `id` is required: a card nothing points at is a card a screen reader never mentions. `popover` chooses between the two mechanisms the stylesheet supports. As a popover the card gets the top layer, light dismiss and anchor positioning, and the trigger is a real button that opens it — which is the answer on a touch screen, where there is no hover. Without it the trigger is a focusable wrapper and the anchor reveals the card on hover or focus, at no interaction cost at all. Neither is right for both cases, so the caller says which; in both, the trigger slot takes a name or a glyph rather than a control, since a control inside would be a second tab stop. The two branches exist because `popover=""` is not an absent popover — the empty value is the `auto` state, so the attribute is written or the tag is written without it.
