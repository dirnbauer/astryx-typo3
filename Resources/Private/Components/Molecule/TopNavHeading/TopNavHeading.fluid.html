TopNavHeading — what the bar is called: a mark, a title, and up to two lines of smaller text around it. Which element it becomes follows from what it does, not from an `as` argument. With an href it is a link home; with `interactive` and no href it is a button — the trigger of a menu or a workspace switcher the caller opens; with neither it is a plain `div`, because a title that goes nowhere should not be in the tab order at all. `data-interactive` is what the stylesheet's cursor and hover tint hang off, and it is only ever written on the two forms that really react. The three lines of text sit in a wrapper of their own rather than directly in the heading. The heading is a flex row — the mark beside the words — so without that wrapper a super, a title and a sub would lay themselves out side by side instead of stacking. The mark goes in the `icon` slot and is expected to be a NavIcon: the heading drops its leading padding when it contains one, so that the round tile lines up with the bar's gutter instead of being pushed in by a padding the tile already carries itself. {f:if(condition: href, then: 'link', else: '{f:if(condition: interactive, then: \'button\', else: \'plain\')}')} {super}{title}{sub} A heading that opens something takes `popovertarget`, which is the browser's own invoker: it opens the panel, closes it on a second click, closes it on Escape and on a click outside, and needs no script. The panel is the caller's — a `.astryx-nav-heading-menu` inside a Popover — because what a workspace switcher lists is not something the bar's title can know.
