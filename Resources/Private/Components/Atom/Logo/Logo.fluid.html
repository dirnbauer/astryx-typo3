Logo — a customer's, a partner's or the site's own mark, in a box of fixed height. Marks are normalised on height, not on width: a wide wordmark and a square emblem set to the same width read as two different sizes, set to the same height they read as one row. `size` picks the height — `sm` 24px, `md` 32px, `lg` 40px, `xl` 48px — and a mark is never wider than five times it, nor than the box it is placed in. The picture comes from the caller, as Avatar's does: only the caller knows the file reference, the crop and the alternative text — which is the organisation's name, never "logo". With no artwork the slot can hold the name itself, and it is then set as type in the same box, which is still proof and better than an empty slot. `tone` is how loud the mark may be: muted (the default) grey and a little faint at rest, so no one brand colour pulls a wall of logos towards itself. Pointing at the link the mark is in, or tabbing to it, brings it to full strength — and, in the light scheme, back to its own colours. In the dark scheme it stays grey: the ink lift that makes a dark mark visible there inverts its colours as well. original the mark's own colours, always. In the dark scheme it sits on a light plate, because a mark drawn for white paper needs white paper under it. ink full strength, its own colours in the light scheme, lifted to light ink in the dark one: the site's own logo in a footer, a single customer mark beside a quote. The ink lift is the chrome's `--g-ink-filter` (brightness(1) in the light scheme, an inversion in the dark one), so marks drawn as dark ink on transparency stay visible on a dark surface. On an accent band there is no colour a mark could safely be drawn in — the band is dark in one theme and near white in another — so there every mark sits on a small plate of the page's surface, as every other surface on the band does. The hover is on the link around the logo, not on the logo: put the Logo inside the Link (or inside a clickable Card), never the other way round.
