Rating — a row of five stars filled to a score. The stars are drawn from the score rather than rounded to it: 4.6 is four stars and six tenths of the fifth, because rounding to a half or a whole star is a claim nobody made. Each star is a square filled from its start edge by a hard gradient stop and clipped to a star outline — no glyph font, no image, and the fill follows the text direction. `value` is the score as an editor typed it, so a decimal comma is read as a point (a German "4,6" is 4.6), and anything that is not a number is nought. `scale` is what the score is out of — 5 unless the source rates out of 10 — and the five stars always show the score as a share of it. What a screen reader hears is the caller's choice, and there are three: - nothing, which is the default: the stars are decoration because the score is printed beside them as text, as it should be — five graphics announcing themselves would say less than "4.6 out of 5"; - the slot: text rendered after the stars ("4/5", "128 reviews"), read as it is shown while the stars stay silent; - `label`: a self-contained rating with no text beside it, announced as one image with that name ("Rated 4.6 out of 5"). `size` is the star: `sm` 16px for a review row, `md` 20px, `lg` 24px for a summary band. Both numbers go into a style attribute, so both are reduced to a number first: a comma becomes a point and f:round casts whatever is left. A scale of nought or less would divide by nought, and falls back to 5. {value -> f:replace(search: ',', replace: '.') -> f:round(precision: 2)} {scale -> f:replace(search: ',', replace: '.') -> f:round(precision: 2)}
