SideNav — the rail down the side of an application: a pinned top, a scrolling middle of sections and items, and a pinned bottom. Not PageSidebar under another name. PageSidebar is a content page's body with a column of sibling links beside it: it takes the menu as an array, renders the main content first in the source and moves the links beside it with a grid, and the whole point of it is that reading order stays content-first. It is a layout with a navigation in it. SideNav is only the navigation, and it is a shell part: it knows nothing about menus, fills itself from SideNavSection and SideNavItem, collapses to a rail of icons, and expects a LayoutPanel or a grid around it to decide where the column is. Use PageSidebar on an article with sibling pages; use SideNav when the rail is the application's own navigation and outlives the page in it. Three regions, and only the middle one scrolls. The top and the bottom are sticky and inherit the rail's background so rows pass under them instead of showing through, which is why they are regions of this component rather than something a caller stacks inside the scroller. `state="collapsed"` is the icon rail. It hides the labels by clipping them, not by removing them, so every row keeps its accessible name — and that is also why a collapsed item needs an icon: with the label clipped it is all that is left to see. The state is server-side; SideNavCollapseButton asks for the other one. `ariaLabel` matters here for the same reason it does on TopNav: an unnamed second `nav` is announced as "navigation" and nothing more, and an application usually has at least two. The tag is branched rather than the id emitted empty: `id=""` is not a valid id, and aria-controls on the collapse button has to have something to point at. The three regions are written twice as a result, which is the cheaper of the two prices — a captured fragment printed raw would hide the slots from anyone reading the file for what this component renders.
