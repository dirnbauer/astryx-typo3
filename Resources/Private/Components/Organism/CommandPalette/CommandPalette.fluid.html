CommandPalette — the ⌘K overlay: a modal holding a query field, a grouped listbox of commands and a line of keyboard hints. It composes Molecule/Dialog rather than writing a second `dialog`. The top layer, the focus trap, Escape, the backdrop and the open/close transition are all already solved there and in 08-overlay.css, and the only thing a palette needs differently is its geometry — it sits near the top of the viewport, is twice as wide and has no padding of its own. That is eight declarations in 11-command-palette.css against sixty lines of restated dialog, and it means a change to how modals animate reaches this one too. No header and no close button: `title` and `closeLabel` are left unset on purpose. A palette is named by its field, the field is the first thing focus lands on, and Escape is both the way out and the last hint in the footer. Opening it is a click on anything carrying `dialogOpen="{id}"` — Button and IconButton both take it — plus whatever binds the keyboard shortcut. What this cannot do is the interesting half. Server-rendered, it is a real modal with a real field and a real, readable list: the browser opens it, traps focus in it and closes it on Escape without a line of our script. It cannot filter the list as the visitor types, it cannot move the highlight with the arrow keys, and pressing Return in the field does nothing — so the rows are listed and inert, and `expanded` and `activeDescendant` are passed through to the field for a script to take over. Rendering rows that looked clickable and were not would be the worse failure; rendering them as the options they are at least tells the truth about what they are waiting for. `listId` defaults off `id` so the field's aria-controls and the listbox's id agree without a caller having to invent two names and keep them matched. Everything goes in Dialog's default slot, including the footer. Dialog does have a `footer` fragment, but it wraps what it is given in the dialog's own padded footer, and a padded strip inside a padded strip is two rules of spacing fighting. The Fluid trap makes the choice cheap to keep: one named fragment in a call and the loose children stop being the default slot altogether, so a call with no fragments at all is the safer shape. {empty}
