ChatMessageList — the transcript: every turn that has already been said, in the order it was said in. An `ol` and not a stack of divs, because order is meaning in a conversation. A reader arriving with a screen reader is told how many turns there are and which one they are on, and reordering the markup would reorder the conversation rather than merely move some boxes. There is no `aria-live` on it, and its absence is the deliberate part. A live region announces what is added to it after the page has settled, which is right for a conversation arriving over a stream and wrong for this one: everything here was rendered with the page. Marking it live makes a screen reader read the entire back-and-forth aloud on load, over the top of whatever the reader was doing — one of the oldest ways to make a chat interface unusable. If this transcript ever gains a script that appends turns, that script adds the live region; the server-rendered markup must not. `ariaLabel` names the list. A list with a name is one a reader can find and skip, and "list, 24 items" on its own says nothing about what the items are. `role="list"` is the one piece of redundant ARIA here and it is doing real work. The stylesheet removes the numbers, and Safari drops list semantics entirely from a list whose markers are `list-style: none` — VoiceOver then announces the turns as loose paragraphs and the count disappears. Stating the role the element already has is the documented way back. The list is the transcript and nothing else — no header, no empty state, no scroll affordance. Those are ChatLayout's, which is what lets a transcript be rendered on its own inside something that is not a chat layout at all.
