Every portrait is cropped server-side to one ratio and framed by AspectRatio, so an upload of any proportion lands in the same box as its neighbours — mixed ratios are the fastest way to make a good team look untidy. The frame is the card's media and bleeds to its edges. A linked person makes the whole card clickable: Card's own clickable state stretches the name's link over the card, so the cell is one link, not two. Each card is the list item itself, and the grid's small items keep two portraits to a row on a phone rather than one per screen. The alt is the person's name rather than empty, even though the name is printed underneath: when a portrait fails to load, a card in a grid of faces should still say whose face is missing, and a reader moving through the page by image hears a roll call rather than a run of blank graphics. The child's own text is deliberately not rendered. A grid where everyone is the same size has room for a name and a role; one person writing three lines makes every other cell look empty.
